# Customer Health Scoring Model

The score is a conversation aid, not an automatic truth. Every change should link to observable evidence and be reviewed with customer context.

## Model

| Component | Weight | Simulated result | Score |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Product adoption | 30 | 18 of 25 machines onboarded | 22 |
| Support health | 25 | Three open tickets; none critical | 19 |
| Engagement | 20 | Administrator active; sponsor review pending | 15 |
| Outcomes and value | 15 | Pilot value demonstrated; rollout incomplete | 12 |
| Sentiment and feedback | 10 | Constructive feedback; neutral-positive sentiment | 8 |
| **Total** | **100** |  | **76** |

## Bands

| Score | Status | CSM response |
|---:|---|---|
| 85–100 | Healthy | Reinforce value, develop champions, explore justified next outcomes |
| 70–84 | Monitor | Maintain a written recovery/adoption plan and review risks weekly |
| 50–69 | At risk | Escalate ownership, increase engagement, and agree corrective milestones |
| 0–49 | Critical | Leadership attention and an immediate recovery plan |

## Guardrails

- Never hide a critical risk inside a high average score.
- Record the evidence date and owner for each component.
- Do not reduce a score merely because a customer gives difficult but useful feedback.
- Review qualitative context alongside the number.
- Use expansion indicators only when the customer is achieving value; do not confuse pressure with success.
