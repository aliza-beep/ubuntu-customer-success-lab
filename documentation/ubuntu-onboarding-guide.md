# Ubuntu Pro Customer Onboarding Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines a simulated onboarding journey for Northstar Apps, a fictional customer adopting Ubuntu Pro across 25 Ubuntu machines. The Customer Success Manager coordinates the journey; qualified customer administrators and support specialists perform technical actions where appropriate.

## Desired customer outcomes

- Clear ownership for all 25 machines.
- Security coverage aligned with the customer’s requirements.
- Administrators understand how to access documentation and support.
- Leadership can see adoption progress, risks, and next actions.
- The customer reaches first value quickly without unsafe production changes.

## Phase 1 — Discover and align

### Kickoff questions

1. What business outcome led to the Ubuntu Pro decision?
2. Which machines are production, development, test, or employee workstations?
3. Who owns security, infrastructure, procurement, and executive sponsorship?
4. What maintenance windows and change controls apply?
5. Which compliance or security requirements matter?
6. How will the customer define a successful first 30 days?

### Outputs

- Confirmed inventory of 25 machines.
- Named customer administrator and executive sponsor.
- Agreed scope, timeline, communication channel, and escalation contacts.
- Baseline measures for adoption, tickets, and customer sentiment.

## Phase 2 — Prepare

- Validate supported Ubuntu versions and machine access with the administrator.
- Confirm the customer’s Ubuntu Pro subscription details through authorised channels.
- Group machines into a small pilot and later rollout waves.
- Agree on backup, change-control, and rollback responsibilities.
- Share the support process and required ticket evidence.

## Phase 3 — Pilot

Start with three representative non-critical machines.

For each machine, the administrator should:

1. Confirm the operating-system version.
2. Confirm the machine appears in the approved inventory.
3. Follow current official Ubuntu Pro attachment guidance.
4. Verify the expected services and security status.
5. Observe the machine through an agreed validation period.
6. Record the result, exception, owner, and next action.

The CSM records progress and business impact but does not request credentials or copy subscription tokens into tickets or documentation.

## Phase 4 — Roll out

Use controlled waves after the pilot is accepted:

| Wave | Machines | Exit condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Pilot | 3 | No unresolved high-impact issue |
| Wave 1 | 7 | Ten total machines validated |
| Wave 2 | 8 | Eighteen total machines validated |
| Wave 3 | 7 | All 25 machines validated |

After each wave, update the dashboard, risks, tickets, customer sentiment, and lessons learned.

## Phase 5 — Enable and adopt

- Demonstrate how administrators check status and find documentation.
- Explain how and when to open a support ticket.
- Provide examples of useful evidence and appropriate severity.
- Introduce relevant products only when they connect to a customer objective.
- Capture questions that should become knowledge-base articles.

## Phase 6 — Review value

At the 30-day review:

- Compare results with the original customer objectives.
- Confirm 25/25 machines are either onboarded or have an agreed exception.
- Review support trends, outstanding risk, and customer feedback.
- Identify potential next steps such as fleet management discussions when justified.
- Document decisions, owners, and dates.

## Definition of done

Onboarding is complete only when the customer confirms the agreed technical validation, understands the support process, accepts remaining exceptions, and can explain the value achieved.
